# EACP load benchmark

MASTER_PLAN §104 measured, under the rules of §105: every figure below comes from the raw results in
[`docs/benchmarks/`](benchmarks/), and the tables between the markers are generated from them
(`go run ./cmd/eacp-bench report --out docs/BENCHMARKS.md docs/benchmarks/baseline-*.json`).
`TestBenchmarksDocMatchesBaseline` fails if the tables are edited by hand. How it works:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-28-load-benchmark-design.md`.

Each PDP ran on a fresh, isolated compose stack (`scripts/bench.sh`). Agents were registered cumulatively
(100, 1,000, 5,000, 10,000); each has its own key, allowlist and THB and USD budget accounts. At each count, load is
open-loop: requests leave at a fixed rate from uniformly random agents, whether or not earlier ones were answered,
and every latency is measured from the moment a request was due. Each step has a 10 s warm-up and a 60 s measurement;
the rate climbs through 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 100, 200, 400 and 800 requests/s and stops after the first step
that is saturated or cannot drain its backlog within 5 minutes. Before each step the tool waits (at most 5 minutes)
for earlier actions to finish, and records how many were still open. One request in twenty replays the request just
before it, and must be answered with the same action. Stage times come from PostgreSQL's audit journal, never from the client's clock.

## Findings

These notes quote the tables above and add no figure of their own. What was measured comes first; each
explanation is labelled as a reading of the code or a possibility, because none was isolated by an experiment.

- **Measured: the action path sustains 40 requests/s with the local PDP and 35 requests/s through the AGT
  sidecar, one rung less at 10000 agents.** The table of maxima gives the rung that failed next to each figure: the
  limit lies between them. Every sustained step completed its new actions (for example 37.9/s at 100 agents and
  40/s, local), with 0 errors, 0 429s and an end-to-end P99 under the 5 s ceiling.
- **Measured: past the limit, the queue, and nothing before it, falls behind.** At the failing rung, admission and
  governance barely move (local, 5000 agents: governance P99 26.4 ms at 40/s and 26.1 ms at 45/s), but queue wait
  jumps from a P99 of 270.1 ms to 171967.7 ms, and completions *fall* (16.0/s at 45/s, against 38.2/s at 40/s) as
  the backlog grows. The AGT run behaves the same way (5000 agents: 33.1/s at 35/s, then 11.5/s at 40/s).
- **Measured: the database does the growing, the worker does not.** The execution worker's mean CPU stays between
  9.3% and 26.8% of one core in every step. PostgreSQL's rises with
  the rate and with the number of registered agents even at a fixed rate: at 25/s its mean CPU is 113.5% at 100 agents
  and 249.6% at 10000 agents (local), and it reaches 442.5% in the saturated local step at 5000 agents and 45/s.
  Admission latency grows with the registry too (local P50 37.0 ms at 100 agents, 89.2 ms at 10000, both at 25/s).
  Sampled lock waits stay small but never zero (DB max lock waits 4 to 14).
- **Reading the code, not measured: why the queue falls behind.** One worker leases actions one at a time
  (`internal/worker/worker.go`, `claim`): each lease is a call to `eacp.claimable_actions`, which ranks the queued
  backlog in fair order, followed by a claim transaction. Its 32 execution slots therefore wait on a serial claim
  path, and a longer queue makes each ranking dearer, which would explain completions falling once a backlog
  forms. Two other contributors are possible and were not isolated: waits on the tenant's audit chain head, which
  every action transaction takes last (the lock waits above), and CPU contention, since Docker was limited to 8
  CPUs and PostgreSQL, the API and, under AGT, the sidecar share them. Batched claims or several workers are the
  likely remedies; neither is measured here.
- **Measured: the AGT sidecar costs governance time and CPU, and one rung.** At 100 agents and 25/s, governance is
  12.5 / 48.2 / 309.6 ms with the local PDP and 80.5 / 90.3 / 98.3 ms through the sidecar, and admission P50 is
  37.0 against 175.6 ms. At 100 agents the sidecar's mean CPU is 126.7% at 25/s and 209.5% at 40/s. The local run's first figures
  are those of the first step on a fresh stack; its next step (30/s) has a governance P99 of 17.4 ms and an
  end-to-end P99 of 376.8 ms.
- **Measured: idempotency and budgets hold under load.** Every step had 0 duplicate keys, and every replay (one
  request in twenty, repeating the request just before it, often while that one was still in flight) was answered
  with its original action: 0 replays answered with another action. `eacp.budget_reserve` averaged between 2.458
  and 7.610 ms per step. A replay's P99 is 22.5 ms at 100 agents and 30/s (local); it grows with the registry like
  admission (80.1 ms at 10000 agents and 25/s).
- **Measured: the LLM gateway sustained 200 calls/s**, the highest step offered, with no errors, in both runs.
  Against a fake provider that answers immediately, client latency at 100/s is 15.3 / 18.6 / 21.8 ms (local) and
  14.9 / 17.3 / 19.5 ms (AGT). At 200/s the tail differs between the runs (P99 350.3 ms local, 45.7 ms AGT); the
  gateway's own ceiling was not reached.
- **Measured: setup** reached 10000 agents from 5000 in 65.2 s (local) and 63.2 s (AGT). Each agent takes 13 API
  calls: its registry, key and budget writes, each approved by a second person.
- **Caveats.** One machine, Docker Desktop limited to 8 CPUs, with the idle development stack running alongside.
  The runs were made from commit `35c593a` with uncommitted documentation changes only: the benchmark code is that
  commit. One tenant and one worker; several tenants, workers or replicas are not measured yet.
